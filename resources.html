Settling In

The practical stuff

Everything you need to get comfortable in Nairobi, beyond the paperwork.

📱

Getting a SIM card

You can get a local SIM (Safaricom, Airtel, or Telkom) from any of their shops or authorised agents shortly after you arrive. You'll typically need your passport for registration.

  • Safaricom is the most widely used network in Kenya and has the best coverage around campus.
  • M-Pesa (mobile money) is essential in Kenya — most payments, from food to fares, go through it. Register for it when you get your SIM.
🏦

Opening a bank account

Most students open an account with a local bank once they have their Student Pass or entry documentation in order, since some banks ask for proof of your immigration status.

  • Bring your passport, admission letter, and proof of address (if requested).
  • Ask the International Students office which banks other Riara students commonly use — this can make the process smoother.
🏠

Housing

Whether you're in university accommodation or looking privately, sort your housing plan before you arrive if possible.

  • Check with Riara's admissions or student affairs office about on-campus or partner housing options.
  • If renting privately, try to view a place (or have someone view it for you) before committing, and confirm the total move-in cost, which is often more than one month's rent.
🚌

Getting around

Nairobi's main forms of transport are matatus (shared minibuses), buses, and ride-hailing apps.

  • Ride-hailing apps like Uber, Bolt, and Little Cab are reliable and a good option while you're still learning your way around.
  • Matatus are the cheapest option once you're familiar with routes — ask current students for the routes near campus.
🍽️

Food

You'll find a mix of campus dining options and nearby local eateries.

  • Local dishes worth trying: ugali, nyama choma, pilau, and chapati.
  • Ask your WhatsApp community for recommendations near campus and student-friendly prices.
🎓

Campus orientation

Riara runs an orientation for new students — make sure you attend, as this is where you'll get official guidance on registration, academics, and student services.

  • Bring your admission documents in case they're needed for registration.
  • Use orientation as a chance to meet other international students in person.

Start your Student Pass early

🛂

Why it can't wait

Studying in Kenya without a Student Pass is an offence, and applications should be submitted at least three months before your course begins. Processing can take a while, so the earlier you have your documents ready, the better.

  • Collect the documents on the Student Pass checklist while you're still at home — some, like a police clearance certificate from your home country, are much harder to get once you've left.
  • Any documents not in English must be translated by an embassy, public notary or recognised institution.
  • Citizens of East African Community countries don't pay the pass fee, but still need to apply.

Travel preparation

✈️

Booking your trip

  • Most international flights arrive at Jomo Kenyatta International Airport (JKIA) in Nairobi.
  • Try to arrive a few days before orientation so you have time to settle, get a SIM card and find your way to campus.
  • If you can, land during the day — it's easier to find transport and get your bearings.
🧳

What to carry in your hand luggage

Keep these with you, not in checked bags — you may need to show them at immigration:

  • Your passport (valid for the length of your stay)
  • Your Riara admission letter
  • Printed or saved copies of your Student Pass documents
  • Your accommodation address and a contact number in Nairobi
  • Any prescription medicine, in its original packaging, with a copy of the prescription
💵

Money for your first days

  • Bring some money you can use straight away for transport, food and a SIM card before you've set up M-Pesa or a local bank account.
  • Let your home bank know you'll be using your card in Kenya, so it isn't blocked.

Plan your accommodation

🏠

Have somewhere to stay before you land

  • Know where you'll sleep on your first night, even if it's temporary, and have the full address saved on your phone.
  • Ask Riara's student affairs or admissions office about on-campus or partner housing options.
  • Ask in the WhatsApp community — current students can tell you which areas are convenient and safe.
  • Be cautious about paying a deposit for a place no one has seen. If you can, have someone you trust view it first.

Immigration & Student Pass

What you need to do

Kenyan immigration law sets out clear responsibilities for every international student. This section covers what applies to you and what's expected before and after you arrive.

International students bring valuable perspective to our campus — and in exchange, Kenyan law sets out a few responsibilities you'll need to stay on top of. Here's what matters most:

  1. Make all immigration-related payments through the eCitizen portal only. Be cautious of anyone posing as an immigration official at points of entry.
  2. Your details must be captured in the border control system before you enter Kenya — entering without this means you've entered the country illegally.
  3. Make sure you receive a valid entry stamp authorizing your stay. This is only issued at official immigration checkpoints, nowhere else.
  4. Entry stamps are valid for a limited period. Check the expiry date and apply for an extension before it lapses, until your Student Pass is issued.
  5. The Student Pass is the single most important immigration document for any international student in Kenya.
  6. Studying at any Kenyan educational institution without a Student Pass is an offence under Section 30(7) of the Kenya Citizenship and Immigration Regulations, 2012.
  7. A Student Pass does not authorize any form of employment, paid or unpaid.
  8. Applications must be submitted at least three months before your course begins.
  9. Applications are processed through the Department of Immigration Services. Riara's International Students office will guide and assist you through this process.
  10. Your Student Pass must be renewed annually, or every two years, depending on the duration approved.

Student Pass

If you're studying in Kenya, you're required to hold a Student Pass. This is issued under Section 30(1) of the Kenya Citizenship and Immigration Regulations (2012), and requires that you've already been accepted into an approved institution — like Riara University.

Application Requirements

  1. A completed, signed, and stamped application (Form 30), authorised by Riara University — required for both new and renewal applications.
  2. A signed cover letter from the university addressed to the Director of Immigration Services, stating your course and its duration.
  3. A signed commitment letter from your sponsor — new and renewal cases.
  4. A copy of your sponsor's national passport — new and renewal cases.
  5. Proof of funds, if you're self-sponsored — new and renewal cases.
  6. A copy of your valid passport's bio-data page — new and renewal cases.
  7. Your current immigration status, if you're already in the country — new and renewal cases.
  8. Two recent colour passport photos — new and renewal cases.
  9. Certified copies of your academic certificates — required for new applications.
  10. Certified transcripts or progress reports — required for renewals.
  11. A Police Clearance Certificate from your home country, or from the Kenya Police Service if you're a resident.

Good to know

  • Any documents not in English must be translated by an embassy, public notary, or recognised institution — for both new and renewal cases.
  • A Student Pass does not permit any form of paid or unpaid employment.
  • Submit your application at least three months before your course starts.
  • Citizens of East African Community countries (Uganda, Tanzania, Rwanda, Burundi) are exempt from the Student/Research/Internship Pass fee — but everyone must still apply and obtain the pass.

Internship / Research Pass

This document is for anyone entering or remaining in Kenya to undertake an internship or academic research. You'll need to already be accepted as an intern or researcher at a Kenyan institution.

Application Requirements

  1. A completed, signed, and stamped application (Form 30), authorised by the host institution — new and renewal cases.
  2. A detailed, signed cover letter from the institution addressed to the Director of Immigration Services, outlining your internship or research topic and its duration.
  3. A signed commitment letter from your sponsor — new and renewal cases.
  4. A copy of your sponsor's national passport — new and renewal cases.
  5. A copy of your CV or resume.
  6. A copy of your valid passport's bio-data page — new and renewal cases.
  7. Your current immigration status, if already in Kenya — new and renewal cases.
  8. Two recent colour passport photos — new and renewal cases.
  9. Certified copies of your basic academic certificates.
  10. A research permit from the National Council for Science and Technology, if undertaking a research programme.
  11. A Police Clearance Certificate from your home country, or from the Kenya Police Service if you're a resident.

Good to know

  • Researchers must include a copy of their research permit from the National Council for Science and Technology.
  • Documents in a foreign language must be translated into English by an embassy, public notary, or recognised institution.

Alien Card

Under Section 56, Part VII of Kenya's Citizenship and Immigration Act (2011), every foreign national residing in Kenya for more than 90 days must register for an Alien Card. Registration certificates are valid for up to two years for general foreign nationals, and up to three years for Permanent Residence Permit holders.

Application Requirements

  1. A completed, signed, and stamped online application (Form 50). You'll print this form once your application is complete.
  2. A copy of your valid passport, to confirm identity and nationality.
  3. A copy of your valid passport's bio-data page — new and renewal cases.
  4. Two recent colour passport photos — new and renewal cases.
  5. A copy of your valid permit and/or pass.
  6. For renewals: your previous Alien Card.

Good to know

  • If your Alien Card is lost, you'll need a valid police abstract as proof.
  • Your registration certificate remains the property of the Kenyan government — it must be surrendered to an immigration officer when you leave Kenya for good, or at your point of departure.
  • A penalty of Ksh 1,000 is charged for every year (or part of a year) that residency went unregistered.

How we can help

Right now, Riara's International Students office assists directly with your Student Pass application. Here's how the process works:

1. Visit the officeCome see Kelvin at the front office to begin your Student Pass application.
2. Get your checklistYou'll receive the full list of documents required for your specific case.
3. We apply for youOnce your documents are ready, Kelvin submits your application to the Department of Immigration Services.
4. Wait for approvalImmigration reviews your application. Kelvin will call you once it's approved to arrange payment.
5. ProcessingAfter payment, your Student Pass is processed. This step can take a while — we'll keep you updated.
